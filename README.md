# Hu Yong Digital Society Thinking Skill

A lightweight AI skill for analyzing digital society, AI, platforms, public discourse, and human subjectivity through a Hu Yong-inspired framework.

This repository is designed as a **thinking tool**, not a quote database.

It helps AI assistants, writers, researchers, strategists, students, and public-interest practitioners move from shallow technology commentary to deeper digital-society analysis.

---

## Why this skill exists

Many analyses of AI, platforms, social media, and digital transformation stop at questions like:

- Is it efficient?
- Is it convenient?
- Will it replace humans?
- Is it good or bad?

This skill pushes the analysis further:

> Technology should not be analyzed only as a tool. It should be analyzed as a force that reorganizes relationships, power, attention, institutions, public discussion, and human subjectivity.

---

## Intellectual orientation

This skill is inspired by Hu Yong's public work and research orientation around:

- digital media and digital society;
- internet culture and network society;
- public discussion;
- platformized society;
- digital economy and management;
- human subjectivity in technological environments.

It does **not** claim that every sentence is Hu Yong's original wording.

Use safe attribution:

> 胡泳式视角 / 受胡泳数字社会问题意识启发 / Hu Yong-inspired digital society analysis

## Core idea

Digital technology is not merely a tool.

It is an environment, infrastructure, and institutional arrangement that reshapes human life.

It reorganizes:

- relationships;
- power;
- attention;
- public discussion;
- institutional arrangements;
- credibility and trust;
- human subjectivity.

## Usage

Copy the skill folder into an AI agent environment that supports Skills-style instruction loading.

The core file is:

```text

```

Recommended prompt pattern:


1. Surface phenomenon
2. Deeper displacement
3. Changed relationships
4. Platform / system power structure
5. Human subjectivity question
6. Strategic, cultural, or ethical implication

---

## Quality bar

A strong output should:

- move beyond simple pros and cons;
- identify a structural displacement;
- map changed relationships;
- discuss power, visibility, trust, or dependency;
- include a human subjectivity question;
- avoid presenting interpretation as direct quotation;
- end with a usable implication.

A weak output usually:

- treats technology as a neutral tool;
- lists generic advantages and disadvantages;
- ignores platform incentives and institutional arrangements;
- confuses popularity with public value;
- ignores who gains power and who loses agency.

---

## Important note

This skill is an interpretive framework inspired by Hu Yong's public intellectual concerns. It does not reproduce his exact words unless a specific source is provided.

When academic precision is required, consult original texts, interviews, books, or lectures, and clearly distinguish:

1. direct quotation;
2. paraphrase;
3. interpretation;
4. extension;
5. application to a new case.
