# Research Map

This file turns the skill into a research-oriented thinking system. It helps users move from a vague digital-society topic to a sharper, layered analysis.

## 1. Intellectual Scope

This skill focuses on digital society analysis, especially the interaction between technology and:

- human subjectivity;
- public discussion;
- media ecology;
- platform power;
- algorithmic visibility;
- institutional change;
- social emotions;
- trust, truth, and authority.

It should not be used as a general technology explainer. It is strongest when the topic involves social consequences, power relations, cultural change, or the meaning of being human in digital systems.

---

## 2. Core Research Questions

Use these questions to deepen any topic.

### A. Displacement

- What has shifted from the edge to the center?
- What used to be invisible but is now visible?
- What used to be stable but is now fluid?
- What used to be mediated by institutions but is now mediated by platforms or algorithms?

### B. Relationship Reorganization

- What relationships are being reorganized?
- Between individuals and institutions?
- Between users and platforms?
- Between experts and laypeople?
- Between creators and audiences?
- Between patients and hospitals?
- Between citizens and public discourse?

### C. Power and Visibility

- Who controls visibility?
- Who defines credibility?
- Who benefits from the new arrangement?
- Who becomes more dependent?
- Who loses interpretive authority?
- Who becomes datafied, ranked, filtered, or excluded?

### D. Public Discussion

- Does the system enable speaking only, or also listening?
- Does it create deliberation or only emotional circulation?
- Does it reward complexity or simplify everything into positions?
- Does it help build public reason, or only attention competition?

### E. Human Subjectivity

- Does the technology expand human agency?
- Does it outsource judgment?
- Does it standardize expression?
- Does it create dependence on optimization metrics?
- Does it allow people to remain reflective, responsible, and dignified?

### F. Social Emotion

- What fear does the technology respond to?
- What fantasy does it promise?
- What anxiety does it amplify?
- What kind of future does it ask people to believe in?

---

## 3. Analytical Axes

Use at least three axes in any substantial output.

| Axis | What to examine | Useful question |
|---|---|---|
| Tool → Environment | Is the technology becoming infrastructure? | What appears to be a tool but is becoming an environment? |
| Expression → Public Discussion | Does more speech create better discourse? | Does the system support listening and deliberation? |
| Efficiency → Agency | What is gained or lost beyond productivity? | Does efficiency enlarge or reduce human possibility? |
| Platform → Institution | How does the platform govern behavior? | What does the architecture reward or punish? |
| Data → Classification | How are people made legible to systems? | Who is categorized, scored, or filtered? |
| AI → Subjectivity | How does AI reshape self-understanding? | What does AI make humans believe about themselves? |
| Trend → Social Emotion | What emotion drives adoption? | Is this technology carrying anxiety, hope, fear, or fantasy? |

Avoid turning every output into jargon. Use concepts only when they clarify the issue.

## 5. Research Workflow

### Step 1: Define the phenomenon

State the surface topic in one sentence.

Example:

> The surface phenomenon is AI-generated workplace writing.

### Step 2: Locate the displacement

State what has shifted.

Example:

> The deeper displacement is from writing as thinking to writing as optimized production.

### Step 3: Map changed relationships

Identify three to five changed relationships.

Example:

- worker and expression;
- employee and manager;
- writer and reader;
- human judgment and machine fluency;
- individual voice and organizational efficiency.

### Step 4: Identify the governing mechanism

Name how the system shapes behavior.

Example:

> The mechanism is not coercion, but default templates, speed expectations, and comparison with machine-generated fluency.

### Step 5: Ask the human question

Translate the issue into subjectivity language.

Example:

> The human question is whether writing remains a method of thinking, or becomes only a method of producing acceptable text.

### Step 6: Produce the implication

End with a cultural, institutional, strategic, or ethical implication.

Example:

> The challenge is not to reject AI writing, but to design practices that keep human judgment visible inside AI-assisted expression.

---

## 6. Quality Bar

A good output should:

- move beyond simple pros and cons;
- identify a structural shift;
- name the changed relationships;
- discuss power, visibility, or dependency;
- include a human subjectivity question;
- avoid pretending that interpretation is direct quotation;
- end with a usable implication.

A weak output usually:

- praises or criticizes technology in general terms;
- only lists advantages and disadvantages;
- treats platforms as neutral channels;
- confuses popularity with public value;
- ignores who gains power and who loses agency;
- quotes or attributes without source discipline.
