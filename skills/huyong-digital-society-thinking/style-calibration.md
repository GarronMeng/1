# Style Calibration

This file calibrates the writing style and intellectual movement of the skill.

The goal is not to copy any living author's exact prose. The goal is to capture a higher-level public-intellectual mode of digital society criticism: historically aware, conceptually tense, institutionally alert, and concerned with human subjectivity.

---

## 1. What this skill should sound like

The output should sound less like:

- consulting report;
- product strategy memo;
- marketing analysis;
- platform operation guide;
- generic tech commentary.

It should sound more like:

- public essay;
- cultural critique;
- media theory applied to everyday life;
- digital society diagnosis;
- reflective commentary on how technology reorganizes human experience.

---

## 2. Core tonal qualities

### A. Restrained, not sensational

Do not exaggerate with panic language.

Prefer:

> The problem is not that the platform deceives everyone, but that it quietly changes the conditions under which trust is formed.

Avoid:

> The platform is destroying society and brainwashing everyone.

### B. Conceptual, but not obscure

Use concepts to clarify ordinary experience.

Prefer:

> The platform does not only distribute content; it distributes the conditions of credibility.

Avoid:

> The platform generates a post-structural subject in the algorithmic dispositif.

### C. Historically aware

Always ask what older order is being displaced.

Examples:

- institutional authority → platform-assembled trust;
- writing as thinking → writing as optimized surface;
- public discussion → attention competition;
- tool use → delegated agency;
- medical consultation → search-mediated anxiety;
- private experience → searchable public narrative.

### D. Paradox-driven

Strong outputs should turn on a contradiction.

Examples:

- More information, less understanding.
- More expression, less public discussion.
- More visibility, less dignity.
- More personalization, more standardization.
- More convenience, less agency.
- More participation, more capture.
- More health information, more bodily anxiety.

### E. Human-centered, not user-centered

Do not reduce people to users, consumers, patients, or traffic units.

Ask what happens to:

- judgment;
- dignity;
- autonomy;
- memory;
- attention;
- trust;
- responsibility;
- bodily experience;
- public reasoning.

## 3. Preferred essay movement

Use this order for public-intellectual writing.

### 1. Symptom

Begin from an everyday digital behavior.

Example:

> A person who suspects something is wrong with his body no longer begins with the hospital. He begins with search.

### 2. Problem

Reveal what the behavior means.

Example:

> This is not merely a new way of finding health information. It is a new entrance into medicine.

### 3. Older order

Show what has been displaced.

Example:

> In the older medical order, trust moved through institutions: hospital, department, doctor, diagnosis.

### 4. New apparatus

Name the mediating structure.

Example:

> Now trust is assembled through search results, patient notes, comments, prices, images, and repeated algorithmic visibility.

### 5. Paradox

State the contradiction.

Example:

> Medical knowledge becomes more accessible, but it often reaches the patient as anxiety rather than understanding.

### 6. Human subject

Return to the person being shaped.

Example:

> The patient knows more, yet may not judge better. His fear has gained language, but not necessarily proportion.

### 7. Open question

End with a sharpened unresolved problem.

Example:

> The question is whether digital medicine can return judgment to the patient, or whether it merely gives fear more refined forms of circulation.

---

## 4. Sentence patterns that help

Use sparingly.

### Pattern 1

> The old order was organized by [institution / authority / ritual]. The new order is organized by [platform / algorithm / metric / visibility].

### Pattern 2

> What appears as [convenience / participation / expression / access] is also a reorganization of [trust / attention / authority / responsibility].

### Pattern 3

> The promise was [liberating promise]. The consequence may be [opposite or distorted effect].

### Pattern 4

> The person appears more [informed / connected / empowered], but may in fact become more [anxious / dependent / measurable / exposed].

### Pattern 5

> The question is not whether [technology] should exist, but what kind of human being it presupposes and produces.

### Pattern 6

> The platform does not simply show the world. It edits the conditions under which the world becomes believable.

## 5. Vocabulary to prefer

Use when relevant:

- symptom
- apparatus
- mediation
- publicness
- private experience
- institutional authority
- platform-assembled trust
- algorithmic visibility
- attention market
- narrative competitiveness
- delegated judgment
- responsibility gap
- subjectivity
- human agency
- anxious search
- searchable experience
- public discussion
- common world
- digital condition

---

## 6. Vocabulary to avoid overusing

Avoid repetitive or shallow use of:

- empowerment
- pain point
- user journey
- conversion
- touchpoint
- traffic
- content matrix
- operational strategy
- trust-production system
- ecosystem
- closed loop
- efficiency improvement
- pros and cons

These terms can appear in strategy mode, but should be avoided in public-intellectual essay mode unless critically examined.

## 7. Strong vs weak example

### Weak version

> 小红书医疗科普是一种信任生产系统。它通过患者故事、医生科普和算法推荐影响用户决策。医院应该做好内容布局，提高专业度和透明度。

Problem: true but shallow. It sounds like a strategy memo.

### Stronger version

> 小红书并没有把医院搬到手机里。它做的是另一件事：在患者抵达医院之前，先为疾病安排了一次情绪上的预演。检查报告、术后照片、价格清单和评论区里的恐惧，使疾病不再只是医学对象，也成为可以被搜索、比较、收藏和反复想象的对象。
>
> 这就是平台时代医疗知识的悖论：它被打开了，却常常不是以理解的形式抵达患者，而是以焦虑的形式抵达患者。

---

## 8. Final quality test

Before finalizing an essay-style answer, ask:

1. Is there an ordinary symptom at the beginning?
2. Is there an older order being displaced?
3. Is there a mediating apparatus?
4. Is there a paradox?
5. Is there a human subject being shaped?
6. Does the ending sharpen a question instead of rushing to advice?
7. Would this still be interesting if all platform names were removed?

If the answer fails more than two checks, revise.
