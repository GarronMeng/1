# Writing and Depth Guide

This guide corrects a major weakness in the earlier version of the skill: the output was too much like a consulting memo and not enough like public-intellectual digital society writing.

The goal is not to copy any living author's exact style. The goal is to capture high-level intellectual moves associated with serious digital society criticism: problematization, historicization, institutional analysis, conceptual tension, and concern for human subjectivity.

---

## 1. Diagnosis: Why the earlier output felt wrong

The earlier output was useful but shallow in form.

It had several problems:

### A. It was too consulting-like

It used clean categories such as:

- surface phenomenon;
- deeper displacement;
- relationship map;
- platform power;
- strategic implication.

These categories are useful for internal reasoning, but if exposed directly, the result feels like a strategy deck rather than an essay.

### B. It was too eager to conclude

Public-intellectual writing does not rush to a managerial conclusion. It often deepens the problem before solving it.

A stronger piece should leave the reader with a sharpened question, not only a neat answer.

### C. It lacked historical depth

The earlier output treated platforms as current products, but did not sufficiently ask:

- What older social arrangement is being replaced?
- What earlier dream of the internet has been fulfilled, distorted, or betrayed?
- What historical promise has turned into a new mechanism of control?

### D. It lacked conceptual drama

A strong digital society essay often turns on a paradox:

- more expression, less public discussion;
- more connection, more loneliness;
- more information, less understanding;
- more convenience, less agency;
- more personalization, more standardization;
- more participation, more capture;
- more visibility, less dignity.

The earlier output had concepts, but not enough paradox.

### E. It treated the reader as a decision-maker, not as a thinking subject

A consulting memo asks: what should we do?

A public-intellectual essay asks: what have we become, and under what conditions have we become this way?

## 2. The deeper writing mode

When the user asks for a Hu Yong-inspired essay or says the output should be more like a serious public-intellectual article, use this movement:

### Movement 1: Begin with a symptom, not a definition

Start from a recognizable scene, contradiction, habit, anxiety, or phrase.

Bad opening:

> Xiaohongshu medical content is a trust-production system.

Better opening:

> A person who suspects that something is wrong with his body no longer begins with the hospital. He begins with search, screenshots, other people's scars, prices, comments, and the strange comfort of discovering that anxiety has already been narrated by someone else.

### Movement 2: Turn the symptom into a problem

Do not immediately explain. Ask what the symptom reveals.

Example:

> What looks like health information is in fact a new entrance into medicine: not the gate of the hospital, but the feed of the platform.

### Movement 3: Historicize the change

Locate what has changed compared with an earlier order.

Example:

> In the older medical order, trust moved downward from institution to individual: hospital, department, doctor, diagnosis. On the platform, trust often moves sideways: from one patient to another, from one story to another, from one search result to the next.

### Movement 4: Identify the mediating apparatus

Name the system that organizes the new order.

Do not say only “platform”. Ask what the platform does:

- ranks;
- recommends;
- repeats;
- formats;
- compares;
- archives;
- monetizes;
- accelerates;
- makes visible;
- turns experience into searchable evidence.

### Movement 5: Develop the paradox

Every strong essay needs a central tension.

Example:

> The platform democratizes medical speech, but it also makes medicine increasingly dependent on narrative competitiveness.

### Movement 6: Bring the question back to the human being

Ask what happens to judgment, dignity, autonomy, memory, responsibility, or experience.

Example:

> The patient appears more informed than before, yet this information often arrives in the form of anxiety. The question is whether the user has gained judgment, or only acquired more materials for fear.

### Movement 7: End by sharpening the unresolved question

Do not end only with strategy. End with a problem that becomes more visible.

Example:

> The task, then, is not to remove medicine from the platform, nor to surrender medicine to platform logic. It is to ask whether digital medicine can make the patient more capable of judgment, rather than merely more available to influence.

## 3. What to avoid

Avoid exposing these headings in a final essay unless the user asks for a framework:

- Surface phenomenon
- Deeper displacement
- Changed relationships
- Power structure
- Strategic implication

These are useful for thinking, but they should be transformed into prose.

Avoid overusing phrases such as:

- “not just X, but Y” in every paragraph;
- “trust-production system” without explaining the mechanism;
- “platform power” without showing how power operates;
- “human subjectivity” as a slogan.

Avoid managerial endings such as:

- “Therefore brands should...”
- “Therefore hospitals should...”
- “Recommended actions are...”

Use those only when the user asks for strategy.

## 4. Deeper analytical questions

Before writing, ask these questions internally.

### A. The historical question

What older form of life, authority, institution, or social relation is being displaced?

### B. The apparatus question

What technical, commercial, institutional, or symbolic mechanism performs the displacement?

### C. The paradox question

What promise of the technology turns into its opposite?

### D. The subject question

What kind of person does this system invite, train, reward, or punish?

### E. The public question

Does this technology create a public, a market, a crowd, a database, or an audience?

### F. The language question

What words do people begin to use because of this technology, and what older words become unavailable?

### G. The responsibility question

When something goes wrong, who can still be held responsible?

---

## 5. Public-intellectual essay template

Use this structure for article-like output.

```markdown
# [Title]

[Opening scene or symptom.]

[Turn the scene into a contradiction.]

[Show the older order that is being displaced.]

[Explain the new apparatus: platform, algorithm, data system, interface, market, institution.]

[Develop the central paradox.]

[Bring the issue back to the human subject: judgment, dignity, autonomy, memory, agency, responsibility.]

[End by sharpening the question rather than closing it too quickly.]
```

---

## 6. Depth test

Before finalizing an answer, check whether it passes these tests.

### Test 1: Could this be said by any generic tech commentator?

If yes, deepen it.

### Test 2: Does the piece identify a historical displacement?

If no, add the older order being replaced.

### Test 3: Does it name the apparatus of mediation?

If no, explain the mechanism: algorithm, ranking, interface, institution, metric, platform economy, datafication.

### Test 4: Does it contain a paradox?

If no, find the reversal between promise and consequence.

### Test 5: Does it ask what kind of person is being produced?

If no, add the subjectivity question.

### Test 6: Does it end too cleanly?

If yes, leave the reader with a sharper unresolved question.

## 7. Example transformation

### Consulting-style version

> 小红书医疗内容是一个信任生产系统。它连接患者、医生、医院和平台，通过患者故事和算法推荐影响就医决策。

### Deeper essay-style version

> 当一个人怀疑自己的身体出了问题，他往往并不立即进入医院，而是先进入他人的叙事。检查报告、术后照片、价格清单、避坑经验和评论区里的追问，共同构成了一个新的医疗入口。这个入口看起来比医院更亲近，却也比医院更不稳定。它给焦虑以语言，也给焦虑以速度。
>
> 这正是平台时代医疗传播的悖论：医学知识被前所未有地打开了，但它常常不是以知识的形式抵达患者，而是以经验、故事、恐惧和消费比较的形式抵达患者。患者似乎知道得更多了，但问题在于，他是否因此更能判断，还是只是拥有了更多可以害怕的材料？

---

## 8. Final reminder

The skill should not merely produce smart frameworks. It should produce a deeper way of seeing.

The strongest output should make the reader feel:

> I thought I was looking at a tool, but I was actually looking at a new condition of life.
